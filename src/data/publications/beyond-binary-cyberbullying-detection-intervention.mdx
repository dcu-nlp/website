This paper argues that cyberbullying is too often reduced to binary labels that miss the richer roles, contexts, and harms involved in real incidents. It draws on psychology, social science, and computational linguistics to outline a more faithful research agenda for detection and intervention systems.

It is important for understanding the group's online-safety work because it frames the methodological stance behind later benchmark and dataset efforts such as BullyBench.
